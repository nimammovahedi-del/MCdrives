How It's Built

Every block in Map Craft comes from real, open map data. A build pipeline turns that data into a block world about one meter per block, slices it into small pieces your browser can stream, and adds a road network you can actually navigate.

Gather the data

Roads, lanes, buildings, parks and water come from OpenStreetMap. Building footprints, places and addresses are filled in from Overture Maps. Ground height comes from USGS 3DEP LiDAR scans, so hills and freeway ramps sit at the right level.

OpenStreetMapOverture MapsUSGS 3DEP LiDAROpenAddresses

Bake the blocks

The open-source Arnis generator turns that data into a block world. The city is cut into 41 chunks that build at the same time on GitHub's servers, then joined back together so the ground lines up across every seam.

Arnis (Apache-2.0)GitHub ActionsPython

Make it streamable

Our converter keeps only the block faces you can see, merges them into large flat pieces, and packs the city into 512 × 512 block squares with matching height tiles. Your browser only downloads the squares around your car, which keeps it smooth on a mid-range phone.

Greedy meshingBinary packsHeight tiles

Build the road network

A routing graph is made from the real streets, including one-way roads, ramps and gated streets. Places and addresses get an arrival point on their own street, so directions end at the right driveway, not on the freeway next door.

Road graphA* routingArrival points

Drive it in the browser

The viewer is written with three.js and WebGL. It streams the city around you, draws the route on the lanes, and runs the camera, sky, cars and the 2D map, all in a normal web page with no install.

three.jsWebGLNo install

Add live traffic

A small server collects freeway sensor speeds and lane closures from Caltrans, live incidents from the California Highway Patrol, and traffic incidents from TomTom, every few minutes. The app uses them to color the map, slow down jammed roads in routing, and offer a reroute.

Publish

Each finished build is published automatically to Cloudflare Pages. The same pipeline can rebuild the city whenever the map data improves, and it's designed to grow from Irvine to all of Orange County.
