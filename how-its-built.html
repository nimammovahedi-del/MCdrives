Where the data comes from

Seven open sources.

Everything on the map comes from public, open data. Pick a layer to see which sources build it. The colored squares show what each source adds.

Pick a layer
MapOpenStreetMap

The world map made by volunteers. It gives us the road network, building outlines and named places.

MapOverture Maps

Open map data from the Overture Maps Foundation. It fills in buildings, places and addresses.

AddressesOpenAddresses

Street addresses collected from county and city records.

LiDARUSGS 3DEP

Laser scans of the ground, so hills and slopes are where they really are.

SensorsCaltrans PeMS

Sensors in the freeway lanes that measure how fast traffic is moving.

IncidentsHighway Patrol

Live crash and hazard reports from the California Highway Patrol.

IncidentsTomTom

Incident reports on city streets, such as closures and roadwork.

Why it runs on a phone

A 4.2 GB county, a few MB at a time.

The whole map is 4.2 GB, far too much for a phone to hold at once. So only the squares around your car are loaded, and they swap as you drive.

Quality check

Checked before it ships.

Before the county goes live, hundreds of photos of the new map go through a multi-step review, and every real problem is fixed and photographed again.

Drag to compare
  1. 743

    Photos taken

    A browser on a build server photographs junctions, bridges, freeways, streets, places and chunk seams.

  2. 148

    Flagged

    A first review pass looks at every photo and flags anything that looks wrong.

  3. 39

    Confirmed

    A second, stricter review checks each flag and keeps only the real defects.

  4. Fixed

    Rebuilt and rechecked

    The cause is fixed in the build tools, the chunks are rebuilt and the same spots are photographed again.
